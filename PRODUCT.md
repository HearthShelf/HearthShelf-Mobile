## Platform

adaptive

## Users

Primary users are people who run their own AudiobookShelf (ABS) server and listen
to their own library. Two situations carry equal weight and both must be designed
for:

- **In the car.** Hands-off listening while driving or commuting. The phone is a
  secondary control surface; the head unit (Android Auto / CarPlay) is where the
  interaction actually happens. Browsing, resuming, and skipping have to work
  without the driver looking.
- **Phone-first, everywhere else.** Walking, chores, falling asleep. The phone
  screen is the primary surface, often held one-handed and often in the dark.

Secondary audiences exist (people invited onto someone else's server, book-club
participants) but they are not what the app is tuned for.

## Product Purpose

HearthShelf Mobile is the phone client for HearthShelf: a warmer, more deliberate
UI/UX layered over a user's own AudiobookShelf server. It signs into the hosted
front door (`app.hearthshelf.com`), connects to the user's linked server, and
plays their audiobook library — on the phone, in the car, and offline.

Success is a listener who can start, resume, and finish books across phone, car,
and web without ever losing their place, and without thinking about the plumbing.

## Positioning

Two things a neighboring ABS client could not truthfully copy:

- **One account, many surfaces.** The hosted front door, the self-hosted server,
  the web app, the phone, and the car share progress, queue, settings, and stats
  through one shared core (`@hearthshelf/core`). Position, queue state, and
  preferences follow the listener across every surface rather than being
  per-client.
- **A social layer ABS lacks.** Book clubs, following, upcoming-release tracking,
  reactions, and timestamped comments turn a private library into something
  shared with other people.

Native car support and visual warmth are real strengths, but they are
differentiators of degree; the two above are structural.

## Operating Context

- **Repo family.** Mobile is one of five repos. `HearthShelf` is the self-hosted
  SPA + Node backend users run; `HearthShelf-WebApp` is the hosted front door
  (`app.hearthshelf.com`) whose auth/connect flow this app ports;
  `HearthShelf-Core` is the shared types + pure logic, consumed here as a git
  submodule at `packages/core`; `HearthShelf-Website` and `HearthShelf-Docs` are
  the marketing and docs sites. A change to shared behavior usually spans repos.
- **Auth chain.** Clerk (Google / Apple SSO) → control-plane grant →
  `/hs/hosted/connect` → per-user ABS token.
- **Car surfaces.** Android Auto is a native Kotlin Media3 `MediaLibraryService`
  (`plugins/hearthshelf-auto`); CarPlay is a native Swift controller + browse
  surface (`plugins/hearthshelf-carplay`). Both are separate native players from
  the phone's, with their own ownership and hand-off rules.
- **Distribution.** Public beta on both platforms — TestFlight (iOS) and Play
  internal test (Android). Releases are tag-driven.
- **Surfaces in the app.** Home, Library, Now/Player, Stats, More; plus book and
  series detail, an ebook reader, search, discover, collections, playlists,
  clubs, following/upcoming, history, notifications, and a deep settings tree.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- **Stack.** Expo SDK 57, React Native 0.86, Expo Router, TypeScript. Audio is a
  native Media3 session engine on Android and a native Swift controller on iOS;
  `react-native-video` is demoted to a secondary role.
- **Offline is a first-class mode, not a failure state.** Downloads, an offline
  SQLite catalog, offline playback resolution, and a pending-progress queue that
  replays local sessions when connectivity returns. Launch must succeed with no
  network at all.
- **Server-authoritative queue.** The self-hosted server computes and persists the
  up-next queue; clients display it. Auto-queue rules live server-side.
- **Reactive theming is shipped functionality.** Theme (dark / light / OLED-flat)
  and accent color are user settings resolved through `ThemeProvider` and
  `src/ui/theme.ts`. No hardcoded palettes.
- **`packages/core` is a submodule and must never be edited in this repo.**
  Changes belong in `C:\code\HearthShelf-Core`, then the pointer is bumped here.
- **Native code survives prebuild.** `android/` is regenerated by
  `expo prebuild`; the source of truth for native behavior is the config plugins
  under `plugins/`.
- **Undecided:** nothing material is recorded as open at capture time.

## Brand Commitments

- **Wordmark (binding).** "Hearth" in gold + "Shelf" in cream, Libre Baskerville,
  always together, never re-colored, never split.
- **Flame logo (binding).** The hearth flame is the app's mark
  (`src/ui/FlameLogo.tsx`, `HearthFire.tsx`).
- **Name and voice.** "HearthShelf" — warm, domestic, unhurried. The metaphor is a
  hearth and a shelf of books, not a media console.
- **Changelog voice (binding, and unusual).** Commit subject lines ship verbatim
  to end users as the changelog. They are written for a non-technical reader —
  no component names, API names, file paths, or jargon.

## Product Principles

1. **Never lose the listener's place.** Position, queue, and progress survive app
   kills, car hand-offs, offline stretches, and surface switches. Anything that
   risks a wrong resume position is a correctness bug, not a polish item.
2. **Design for the scene, not the screenshot.** The two real scenes are a driver
   who cannot look and a person in a dark room holding the phone one-handed.
   Every surface is judged in one of those.
3. **Offline and car are parity requirements.** A surface is not finished until it
   behaves correctly with no network and, where it has a car equivalent, in the
   car. Neither is a degraded fallback.
4. **Warm, not clinical.** The hearth metaphor is the point. Warmth is earned
   through material, type, and restraint — never through decoration that costs
   legibility in a dark room or a moving vehicle.
5. **The user's theme wins.** Every surface renders correctly in dark, light, and
   OLED-flat, at the user's accent color and text size. A surface that only looks
   right in one palette is unfinished.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No formal compliance target is set. The named, real user need is **low-vision and
night listening**: people read this app in the dark, sometimes at large text
sizes. OLED/flat theme legibility, dim-room contrast, and large-type layout
survival are requirements, not options.

Existing habits to preserve: screen-reader labeling (`accessibilityLabel` /
`accessibilityRole` are used across ~50 files) and reduce-motion respect
(`useReducedMotion`). Car surfaces carry their own glanceability burden under
Principle 2.
